Where the pieces come from

I grew up around stories. Iranian myths, Armenian and Assyrian history, the patterns people have carried for centuries.

Every piece here starts as a drawing. I work out who the figure is and what has to be kept, long before I think about the metal. The question I keep asking is whether the story is still there once the piece is small enough to wear.

A piece can carry a myth and still sit quietly on a hand.

The Shamiram necklace takes its name from the Assyrian and Armenian queen. The drawing came first — the headdress, the double chain, the star at the chest — and the finished pendant keeps all three.

Pourandokht was a Sasanian queen. I wanted her crown to feel regal without turning into a costume piece, so the wings are folded in close and the stones sit low.

Dream of Flying is a damselfly caught in a pair of human hands, still dreaming of freedom. Of everything in this collection, it says the most about how I feel about what we take from nature.

These pieces were made with Farhadian Gold. I design through to the final model, and the casting is done in their workshop.
